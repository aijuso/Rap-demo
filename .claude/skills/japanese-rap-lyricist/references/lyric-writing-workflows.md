# Japanese rap lyric-writing workflows

Use this reference to choose a composition route, develop hooks and verses, and
revise a draft through performance. For beat placement and breath design, read
`flow-and-cadence.md`. For acceptance criteria, read `evaluation-rubric.md`.

## Source policy

- Prefer an artist's own account, an official interview, a lecture, or a
  phonetics paper over unsourced "rules."
- The workflows below are abstractions from sources, not universal laws.
- Never reproduce a living artist's signature wording or ask for a close
  imitation. Convert techniques into neutral constraints, then write original
  material.
- Do not copy published lyric lines into prompts, examples, or outputs. Describe
  the device instead.

## Choose a starting mode

Do not force every song through the same route. Pick one primary mode and, if
useful, one rescue mode.

| Mode | Begin with | Best when | Main risk | Rescue move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning-first | Claim, scene, conflict, or lived event | The user has something specific to say | Expository prose with no musical life | Speak the idea over the loop and rewrite around its natural stresses |
| Rhyme-first | A compact rhyme family or sound cluster | Discovery and word association matter | Semantic drift and filler | Keep only pairs that open a useful image or argument |
| Flow-first | Nonsense syllables, mouth-feel, stress pattern | The beat strongly suggests a pocket | Empty phonetic display | Replace syllables with concrete language without changing the rhythmic skeleton |
| Hook-first | A repeatable phrase, cadence, or melodic cell | The song needs an immediate identity | Verses merely paraphrase the hook | Give each verse a new scene, consequence, or counterargument |
| Experience/note-first | Memory, observation, overheard phrase, image | Autobiographical specificity matters | A scrapbook with no through-line | Limit imported notes to 1–3 seeds and write connective material fresh |
| Character/story-first | Speaker, goal, obstacle, setting, turn | Narrative or fictional framing matters | Plot summary instead of rap | Dramatize decisive moments and let details imply backstory |
| Collage/free-association | Several claims or punchline-sized thoughts | Cypher, 64-bar, or release-valve formats | Randomness without escalation | Order fragments by energy, image, or recurring motif |
| Deliberate sparse-rhyme | Cadence, syntax, image, and assertion | A plain line must land harder than ornament | Reads like ordinary prose | Make placement, pause, consonance, or repetition carry the music |

R-指定 explicitly distinguishes meaning-first, rhyme-first, and flow-first
approaches, says he changes methods when stuck, and discusses intentionally
leaving a passage unrhymed. Treat this as evidence for multiple valid starting
modes, not a command to emulate his voice:
<https://miyearnzzlabo.com/archives/66634>

KREVA describes rhyme as a trigger for ideas rather than decoration alone:
<https://www.joqr.co.jp/qr/article/86327/>

R-指定 also describes a hook emerging from mouth-feel and flow before the
concept became explicit:
<https://www.billboard-japan.com/special/detail/4723>

## Establish the song contract

Before drafting, write a compact contract:

```yaml
format: song | 16-bar verse | 64-bar performance | battle-style exercise
speaker: first-person role and distance from the real author
listener: who is being addressed
time_place: concrete present scene
core_tension: desire versus obstacle
vibe: 2-3 adjectives
primary_mode: meaning | rhyme | flow | hook | notes | story | collage
turn: what changes by the end
hook_job: thesis | emotional release | question | slogan | contrast | none
prohibited: copied lines, named-artist imitation, unsupported biography
```

The contract is a control surface, not prose to paste into the lyric. If a line
does not serve it, either cut the line or consciously update the contract.

## Build a bounded source bank

Gather material before writing:

1. Five concrete nouns from the actual setting.
2. Three actions observable by a camera.
3. Two sensory details.
4. One contradiction in the speaker.
5. One private fact or unusual observation, if supplied by the user.
6. One image that can recur with changed meaning.
7. A small rhyme bank for only the most important anchor words.

Bonbero says he records memories, observations, and rhyme material, but may use
only one to three lines from the notes because building the whole song from the
bank can scatter its coherence. Apply that as a retrieval limit:
<https://fnmnl.tv/2022/12/13/149988>

Do not invent personal history to make a lyric seem authentic. If the user has
not supplied facts, write a fictional speaker and label that choice internally.

## Draft in passes

### Pass 1: rhythmic skeleton

Loop the beat or use a declared BPM and meter. Hum, scat, or speak neutral
syllables. Mark:

- likely entrances;
- stressed landing points;
- pauses;
- bar-ending anchors;
- places where syntax can cross the bar;
- one contrast in density.

Do not optimize rhyme yet. The goal is a speakable contour.

### Pass 2: semantic spine

Write one sentence for each section:

- opening state;
- complication;
- turn or reveal;
- changed closing state.

For a non-narrative verse, replace plot with argument:

- claim;
- evidence or scene;
- complication;
- sharper restatement.

### Pass 3: lexical fill

Replace neutral syllables with concrete words while preserving the strongest
stress pattern. Prefer verbs, objects, locations, and sensory evidence over
abstract self-description.

Use rhyme to connect or contrast meanings. Reject a rhyme if it requires:

- unnatural word order;
- a fact the speaker would not know;
- a register that breaks the voice;
- repeated empty claims;
- a pronunciation that only works on paper.

### Pass 4: compression and surprise

For every bar, ask:

- Can one clause do the work of two?
- Is there a more specific noun or verb?
- Does the line add a new image, fact, turn, or sound event?
- Is the most important word placed where the performance can emphasize it?
- Would removing the rhyme improve the line?

ZORN describes repeated work on word choice, order, addition, subtraction, and
overall balance, alongside flow, voice, and beat:
<https://a-files.jp/zorn-interview.html>

## Hook construction

Choose the hook's job before judging it.

### Thesis hook

Compress the core claim into a phrase whose meaning is stable under repetition.
Verses supply evidence and complications rather than paraphrases.

### Emotional-release hook

Let the verses carry information and the hook release tension through a simpler
vocabulary and wider rhythmic space.

### Question or unresolved hook

Repeat the central doubt. Each return should sound different because the verse
has changed its context.

### Unexpected-phrase hook

Start from a strange, funny, or apparently provisional phrase with strong
mouth-feel. Preserve useful oddness; do not polish every irregularity into a
generic slogan.

PUNPEE discusses songs arising from sudden, unusual hooks, provisional hooks
surviving into final versions, and humor or strangeness being valuable:
<https://eyescream.jp/music/125820/>

KREVA describes revisiting handwritten morning pages to find a hook seed and
allowing an important line to remain unrhymed:
<https://realsound.jp/2023/09/post-1437661.html>

### Hook audit

A hook passes only if:

1. It can be recalled after one or two readings.
2. Its rhythm differs enough from the verse to mark a section.
3. Repetition adds force rather than exposing empty wording.
4. It contains no generic placeholder such as an unsupported claim of
   greatness.
5. Its final stressed word is performable.

Do not add a hook to formats that do not call for one. Red Bull's 64 Bars
description contrasts a typical hook/verse form with an uninterrupted,
hookless performance:
<https://www.redbull.com/jp-ja/projects/64barsjp-s2/64bars-s2-introduction>

## Punchlines without punchline inflation

A punchline is a local payoff. It may resolve a setup, reverse an assumption,
compress a comparison, or land a memorable truth. It is not merely a rhyme at
the end of a bar.

Build one with:

```text
setup: establish an expectation
bridge: retain enough shared meaning for the turn to be legible
turn: switch frame, scale, referent, or consequence
landing: place the decisive word on a strong, audible point
```

Require at least two functions from a candidate line:

- semantic turn;
- concrete image;
- character revelation;
- structural payoff;
- sound payoff;
- emotional consequence.

Do not force every bar to be quotable. Some artists prioritize whole-verse flow
or meaning:

- $MOKE OG emphasizes pleasant flow across the track:
  <https://www.redbull.com/jp-ja/rasen-12-smokeog>
- FARMHOUSE says the verse's meaning works as a whole rather than through one
  extractable line:
  <https://www.redbull.com/jp-ja/rasen-21-farmhouse>
- ANARCHY discusses a looser format that places wanted thoughts at the phrase
  or punchline level without one binding theme:
  <https://www.redbull.com/jp-ja/64bars-anarchy>

Select either `payoff-dense`, `groove-first`, or `whole-arc` as the intended
mode. Do not score one mode by another mode's goal.

## Story and point of view

### Scene card

For each scene, specify:

```yaml
where:
when:
who_is_present:
speaker_wants:
obstacle:
visible_action:
sensory_detail:
information_revealed:
exit_image:
```

Use actions and objects to carry explanation. A lyric should not sound like an
essay broken into bars.

宇多丸 describes converting explanation into emotional expression, grounding
the point of view in the affected person, and rewriting in response to producer
feedback:
<https://ikinobirubooks.jp/series/sasaki-tomoko/1998/>

PUNPEE discusses treating guests as characters within a script or story and
placing real experience inside fictional devices such as time travel or
alternate worlds:
<https://eyescream.jp/music/125820/>

Awich discusses developing a concept from real experience, adjusting language
choice to point of view, and writing while a track develops:
<https://qetic.jp/interview/awichep-pickup/297853/>

### Narrative quality checks

- The speaker wants something, even in a reflective song.
- At least one fact, interpretation, or power relation changes.
- Pronouns and address remain traceable.
- Flashbacks and imagined scenes have a clear entry cue.
- The final image is earned by an earlier detail.
- A guest verse has a dramatic role, not just a second résumé.

## Provisional recording loop

Do not treat the page as the final medium.

1. Record the complete draft over the intended beat or a metronome.
2. Listen once without reading.
3. Mark only audible failures: swallowed word, late landing, forced
   pronunciation, breath break, flat energy, unclear referent, or section that
   feels too long.
4. Repair the smallest failed unit.
5. Re-record the repaired line with the line before and after it.
6. If the local change shifts stress or meaning, propagate the edit to adjacent
   lines.
7. Listen again from the section start.
8. After two unsuccessful local cycles, rewrite the four-bar unit.
9. If the contract itself is wrong, permit a full reset.

VIGORMAN describes writing across days, recording, replacing weak sections, and
then revising neighboring lines:
<https://www.redbull.com/jp-ja/64bars-vigorman>

Mummy-D emphasizes performance and recording rather than writing only through
intellect, and describes discarding a weak recorded verse:
<https://fika.cinra.net/article/201910-mummyd_kngsh>

CHICO CARLITO describes discarding and rebuilding an entire 64-bar draft:
<https://www.redbull.com/jp-ja/podcast-episodes/behind-the-bars-japan-red-bull-64-bars-s1-e8>

ISSUGI notes that a format much longer than his normal work required multiple
sessions over roughly a month. Scale the review cadence to the task:
<https://www.redbull.com/jp-ja/64bars-issugi>

## Cooling and originality pass

After a complete take, stop editing briefly when the deadline permits. Then:

1. Read only the lyric and test semantic continuity.
2. Listen without the lyric and test intelligibility and energy.
3. Compare the output to the contract.
4. Search distinctive aphorisms or punchlines for accidental duplication.
5. Replace lines that are merely familiar rap formulas.
6. Confirm that no requested artist has been imitated through signature
   phrases, biography, ad-libs, or a recognizable cluster of mannerisms.

Bonbero describes spending substantial time deciding whether a completed song
is actually good and checking a possible line online after suspecting prior
use:
<https://fnmnl.tv/2022/12/13/149988>
